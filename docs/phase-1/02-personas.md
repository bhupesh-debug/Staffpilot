# StaffPilot — Customer Personas (Phase 1)

**Status:** Working hypotheses to validate in discovery — every persona below is a hypothesis, not a researched fact, until interview evidence confirms it.
**Date:** November 22, 2026

Personas are written for the 20–100-worker segment in the United States, New York first. Each persona includes pains, jobs-to-be-done, buying triggers, and how StaffPilot should reach them. Where a claim is unverified it is marked [UNVERIFIED].

---

## Persona 1 — The Staffing Agency Owner

**Who:** Founder/owner of a small IT or finance/accounting staffing firm, 20–100 consultants placed at clients. Often still involved in sales and delivery personally.

**Firm profile:** 20–100 placed workers; a handful of internal staff (recruiters, one ops/payroll person); revenue driven by bill-rate minus pay-rate margin.

**Pains**
- Margin visibility is poor: bill vs. pay rates live in spreadsheets; nobody can say this week's true margin without manual work.
- Timesheet and billing disputes eat the owner's time at month-end.
- Losing a consultant or a client because onboarding/assignment admin is slow or sloppy.
- Can't tell whether the firm is growing profitably or just growing headcount.
- Payroll preparation is a scramble every cycle; errors surface after invoices go out.

**Jobs-to-be-done**
- "Show me my margin by client, consultant, and week — without asking my ops person to build a spreadsheet."
- "Get timesheets approved and invoiced on time, every cycle, with no chasing."
- "Onboard a new consultant and get them billing within days, not weeks."
- "Know exactly what payroll preparation will cost me before I run it."

**Buying triggers**
- A billing error or missed invoice that cost real money.
- Headcount crossing ~30–40, where spreadsheets stop working.
- A key ops employee leaving and taking the spreadsheet knowledge with them.
- A client demanding faster, cleaner invoicing or audit-ready timesheet records.

**How to reach them:** Direct outreach (email/LinkedIn) referencing a specific operational pain; referrals from other small-agency owners; local NY staffing community presence [UNVERIFIED — channel effectiveness to test].

**What they care about most:** Margin, cash flow, and time. **What they don't care about:** Feature lists; enterprise jargon.

**Decision role:** Economic buyer and final sign-off. Budget authority: yes.

## Persona 2 — The Recruiter (Internal or Agency)

**Who:** Recruiter placing IT or finance/accounting consultants at client sites; measured on placements and fill speed.

**Pains**
- Loses track of which consultants are available, on assignment, or rolling off — and when.
- Submits candidates without knowing the current bill/pay-rate band for a role.
- Chases timesheets and onboarding paperwork instead of recruiting.
- Handover to ops/payroll is manual and error-prone; placements stall after the offer.

**Jobs-to-be-done**
- "Tell me who is available, at what rate, and when they roll off."
- "Place a candidate and hand them to ops without re-keying everything."
- "Never lose a placement because paperwork sat in someone's inbox."

**Buying triggers**
- A lost placement caused by slow handover or missing rate information.
- A client asking for candidate pipelines the recruiter can't produce quickly.
- Growing req volume that spreadsheets can't track.

**How to reach them:** They rarely buy software themselves — they influence the owner. Win them with a demo that shows availability and rate visibility in one screen. Recruiters who love the tool become internal champions.

**Decision role:** Influencer / champion. Budget authority: no (usually).

## Persona 3 — The Payroll Manager / Payroll & Billing Lead

**Who:** The one person (sometimes part-time, sometimes wearing multiple hats) who runs timesheets, billing, and payroll preparation each cycle. In a 20–100-worker firm this is often a single ops/payroll lead, not a department.

**Pains**
- Re-keying hours from email, chat, and paper into billing and payroll-prep tools.
- Chasing late timesheets and client approvals every single cycle.
- Reconciling billing vs. payroll-prep figures by hand; finding errors after the fact.
- No audit trail: when a client disputes hours, there is no clean record of who approved what and when.
- Payroll-prep figures that don't tie to approved hours, forcing rework.

**Jobs-to-be-done**
- "Collect every timesheet in one place, approved, before the billing deadline."
- "Generate invoices from approved hours without re-typing anything."
- "Produce a payroll-preparation estimate that ties exactly to approved hours and rates."
- "Answer a client dispute in minutes with an approval audit trail."

**Buying triggers**
- A payroll-prep error that delayed pay or caused a client credit.
- Month-end close taking days instead of hours.
- An audit or client request for timesheet approval records they can't produce.

**How to reach them:** Practical demos using their own workflow language (cycle, approval, invoice, estimate). They evaluate on "does this save me hours per cycle" — measure it in the trial.

**Decision role:** Primary user and evaluator; strong influence on the buy decision. Budget authority: usually no, but a veto if the tool makes their job harder.

**Critical note:** This persona must be told — clearly and repeatedly — that StaffPilot is payroll *preparation* (estimates), not payroll processing. Overselling here destroys trust.

## Persona 4 — The C2C Vendor Manager

**Who:** Manages corp-to-corp (C2C) vendor relationships: consultants employed by third-party vendors and placed at the firm's clients. Common in IT staffing.

**Pains**
- Vendor consultant hours, rates, and vendor invoices tracked in separate spreadsheets per vendor.
- Vendor payment terms and client billing terms misaligned — cash-flow gaps.
- No single view of which vendor consultants are on which assignments, at what margin.
- Compliance/documentation risk: vendor agreements, rate confirmations, and consultant documents scattered across email.
- Disputes over vendor hours with no shared approval record.

**Jobs-to-be-done**
- "Show me every vendor consultant, their assignment, rate, and vendor — in one place."
- "Approve vendor hours the same way I approve direct consultants' hours."
- "Know my margin on vendor-supplied consultants, not just my direct ones."
- "Keep vendor documents and rate confirmations organized and retrievable."

**Buying triggers**
- A vendor billing dispute with no clean record.
- Vendor headcount growing past what spreadsheets can track.
- A client audit asking for vendor consultant records.

**How to reach them:** Outreach that names the C2C workflow specifically — generic "staffing software" messaging will not land. Discovery must confirm how they currently track vendors before any demo.

**Decision role:** User and influencer; in vendor-heavy firms, a co-buyer with the owner.

**Hard boundary:** StaffPilot never auto-classifies C2C consultants as independent contractors. Classification is the customer's legal determination. The product records the engagement type the customer enters and keeps vendor documentation organized — it does not advise on classification.

## Persona 5 — The Small Consulting-Firm Ops Lead

**Who:** Operations lead (often also office manager / COO in a small firm) at a professional-services or consulting firm with 20–100 consultants on client projects. Not a pure staffing agency — the firm sells projects and staff augmentation together.

**Pains**
- Project staffing, utilization, and billing live in different tools; nobody sees the whole picture.
- Consultants split across projects with no clean allocation or utilization view.
- Project billing is manual and lags project delivery.
- Onboarding a consultant onto a project involves too many handoffs.

**Jobs-to-be-done**
- "Show me utilization and project margin in one view."
- "Staff a project from available consultants without a spreadsheet hunt."
- "Bill project hours as soon as they're approved — not at month-end."
- "Run payroll preparation from the same approved hours I already billed."

**Buying triggers**
- A project that looked profitable but wasn't, discovered too late.
- Utilization dropping and nobody noticing until the quarter ends.
- Growth to the point where the ops lead can't hold the whole picture in their head.

**How to reach them:** Messaging around utilization and project margin, not "staffing." They respond to operational clarity, not recruiting features.

**Decision role:** Evaluator and often co-buyer with the firm's principal. Budget authority: sometimes.

## Cross-persona notes

| Persona | Buys? | Uses daily? | Top pain | Key message |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Agency Owner | Yes | Weekly | Margin blindness | "See your margin, every week." |
| Recruiter | No — influences | Daily | Availability/rate blindness | "Know who's free, at what rate." |
| Payroll Manager | No — evaluates/vetoes | Daily | Rework and chasing | "One approved hour, billed and prepped." |
| C2C Vendor Manager | Influences/co-buys | Daily | Vendor chaos | "Every vendor consultant, one view." |
| Consulting Ops Lead | Sometimes | Daily | Utilization blindness | "Utilization and margin, one view." |

**Validation plan:** Each persona must be confirmed or corrected by at least 3 discovery interviews before Phase 2 messaging is finalized (see `09-discovery-questions.md`). If interviews contradict a persona (e.g., the payroll role doesn't exist as a separate job in most target firms), the persona is revised — not defended.
