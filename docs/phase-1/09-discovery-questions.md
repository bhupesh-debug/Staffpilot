# StaffPilot — Customer Discovery Interview Script (Phase 1)

**Status:** Interview guide. Use as written; adapt wording to the conversation, not the answers.
**Date:** November 22, 2026
**Format:** 30-minute video/phone call. Ask permission to take notes. Do not pitch during the interview — the first 25 minutes are listening. The last 5 minutes may include a brief concept check (Section F) and the referral ask.

**Ground rules for the interviewer**
- Ask open questions; do not lead ("Don't you find timesheets painful?" is leading — ask how timesheets work today).
- Ask for the last time something happened, not for opinions ("Walk me through your last payroll cycle" beats "Is payroll hard?").
- Record numbers when offered (hours, dollars, headcount) and mark them as self-reported.
- Never promise a feature, a price, or a timeline during the interview.
- Never claim StaffPilot does payroll processing, tax filing, or anything at the boundary. If asked, say: "StaffPilot prepares payroll estimates from approved hours; your payroll processor runs the actual payroll."

---

## A. Context (5 min)

1. Tell me about your firm — what do you do, how many people work there, and how many consultants or employees do you place or manage?
2. What is your role, and what part of staffing operations / payroll / billing do you personally handle?
3. How many workers are on assignment right now, and how many clients are they spread across?
4. How has headcount changed over the past year — growing, flat, shrinking?
5. What does a typical week look like for you, in terms of the operational work (not sales or recruiting)?

## B. Current workflow — timesheets and approvals (5 min)

6. Walk me through how a consultant's hours get from the consultant to an approved timesheet today. What tools are involved?
7. Who approves timesheets — a manager, the client, or both? How does that approval actually happen (email, portal, chat, paper)?
8. How long does the timesheet-to-approval cycle usually take? What happens when it's late?
9. How often do timesheets come back wrong or disputed, and what does fixing one cost you in time?
10. What happens when a client disputes hours after you've already invoiced?

## C. Current workflow — billing and margin (5 min)

11. How do you build an invoice today? What source do the hours and rates come from?
12. How do you know your margin on a consultant or a client — bill rate minus pay rate — and how often do you actually look at it?
13. Walk me through your last billing cycle: how long did it take, and where did it get stuck?
14. Have you ever lost money on a billing error or a late invoice? What happened? (Ask gently; don't press if they decline.)
15. How do you track bill rates vs. pay rates today — one place, or several?

## D. Current workflow — payroll preparation (5 min)

16. Once hours are approved, how do you prepare payroll? Who does it, and what tools do they use?
17. Who actually runs your payroll (a processor, an accountant, in-house)? How do the prepared figures get to them?
18. What goes wrong most often in payroll preparation — wrong hours, wrong rates, missing data, something else?
19. How long does payroll preparation take per cycle, in hours, for the person who does it?
20. Is there an audit trail today for who approved which hours and which rates were used? If a client or auditor asked, could you produce it?

## E. C2C vendors, tools, and money (5 min)

21. Do you work with C2C vendors (consultants employed by a third-party vendor)? If so, how do you track their hours, rates, and vendor invoices today?
22. What tools do you currently pay for to run this workflow (spreadsheets, payroll software, accounting, anything else)? What do they cost per month, roughly?
23. How much staff time per week goes into timesheets, billing, and payroll preparation across your firm? (Best estimate is fine.)
24. If you could wave a wand and fix one part of this whole workflow, which part would it be — and what would "fixed" look like?
25. What have you tried before to fix it? What happened — why didn't it stick?

## F. Concept check and willingness to pay (5 min)

26. [Brief concept, read verbatim:] "StaffPilot is one platform where assignments, bill and pay rates, timesheets, client approvals, billing, and payroll-preparation estimates live together — one approved hour flows to the invoice and to the payroll estimate, with an audit trail. It prepares payroll estimates; it does not move payroll funds or file taxes." — What is your first reaction?
27. Which part of that would help you most? Which part matters least?
28. Who else in your firm would need to be involved in deciding on a tool like this?
29. Our proposed pricing is a monthly base fee plus a per-worker monthly charge — for example, around $249 a month for a 50-worker firm on our middle tier. Is that in a range you'd consider, too high, or surprisingly low? What would make it worth it?
30. What would have to be true for you to try a 14-day free trial with your real data?
31. What would stop you from adopting a tool like this, even if it worked as described?
32. If we built a pilot version for firms like yours, would you be open to a 30-day guided pilot with your real data, in exchange for a feedback conversation at the end?

## G. Close (2 min)

33. Who else should I talk to — another agency owner, an ops lead, a payroll person — who runs this workflow and would have a view?
34. Is there anything about how you run staffing operations, billing, or payroll preparation that I haven't asked about but should understand?
35. May I follow up with you when we have something to show? What is the best way to reach you?

---

## After the interview — capture immediately (within 1 hour)

- Firm, persona, headcount, client count.
- Current tools and rough monthly cost (self-reported).
- Top 3 pains, in their words (quote verbatim where possible).
- The "one thing they'd fix" (Q24) — this is the lead value-prop signal.
- Willingness-to-pay signal (Q29) and trial openness (Q30, Q32).
- Referral names (Q33).
- Persona confirmation: does this person match the persona we assumed? What should change?
- One-line summary: "This firm runs [workflow] in [tools], loses [time/money] on [pain], would pay [signal] for [fix]."

## Interview targets

- Minimum 15 interviews in the first 30 days; 35 by Day 90 (see `08-90-day-launch-strategy.md`).
- At least 3 interviews per persona before that persona is treated as confirmed.
- Stop interviewing a persona segment once answers repeat (saturation) — and say so in the notes rather than padding the count.
