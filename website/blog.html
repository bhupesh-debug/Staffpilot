Workflow

One approved hour, billed and prepped: the loop that replaces five spreadsheets

November 19, 2026 · 5-minute read

Most small staffing firms already run the same loop every week: a consultant logs hours, a manager approves them, a client approves them, someone builds an invoice, and someone prepares payroll. The problem is not the loop — it is that the loop runs in five places. Hours live in email, approvals in a reply-all thread, rates in a spreadsheet, invoices in an accounting tool, and payroll preparation in a second spreadsheet built from the first.

When one approved hour feeds both billing and payroll preparation, three things change. First, billing and payroll prep cannot drift apart, because they are built from the same hours. Second, a disputed hour has an answer: who approved it and when. Third, margin is visible during the week, not reconstructed at month-end. That is the whole idea behind StaffPilot’s core loop — timesheet → manager approval → client approval → invoice → payroll-preparation estimate — and the test of any tool that claims to run it is simple: enter an hour once, and count how many times you have to re-type it.

Payroll preparation

Payroll preparation is not payroll processing — and why the distinction matters

November 12, 2026 · 4-minute read

“Payroll preparation” and “payroll processing” sound like the same thing. They are not, and blurring them is the single biggest trust risk for a staffing firm choosing software.

Preparation means computing what payroll would look like from approved hours and pay rates, so you can review it and hand it to the company that actually runs your payroll. Processing means moving the money: direct deposits, tax filing, tax deposits, withholdings. StaffPilot does the first and is explicit that it never does the second. It does not store Social Security numbers or bank details, and it labels every payroll figure an estimate.

If a vendor ever implies it runs payroll while only preparing estimates — or asks for bank details it does not need — treat that as a reason to walk away, whoever the vendor is. Ask any payroll tool three questions before you buy: do you move funds, do you file taxes, and do you store SSNs or bank details? The answers should be short, specific, and in writing.

C2C & vendors

Managing C2C vendor consultants without losing the margin — or the paperwork

November 5, 2026 · 4-minute read

Vendor-supplied consultants create a second books problem: their hours, rates, and invoices are tracked per vendor, often in a separate spreadsheet each, while the client is billed from a different one. The margin on a vendor consultant is real money, but it is the last margin a firm can see clearly.

The fix is unglamorous: put vendor consultants on the same assignments, the same timesheet workflow, and the same approval record as direct consultants, with the vendor rate visible next to the bill rate. Then vendor invoices can be checked against approved vendor hours, and a vendor billing dispute has a shared record instead of two competing spreadsheets.

One boundary matters more than the workflow: no software should auto-classify a C2C consultant as an independent contractor. Classification is your legal determination, made with your own counsel. A tool should record the engagement type you enter and keep the documentation organized — nothing more.
